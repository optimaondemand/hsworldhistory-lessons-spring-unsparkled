Willy-Nicky Telegrams (1914)
The Great War
💡 BEFORE YOU READ

In the last days of July 1914, Kaiser Wilhelm II of Germany and Tsar Nicholas II of Russia, who were cousins, sent each other a series of telegrams. They signed them "Willy" and "Nicky." Each man hoped to stop the slide to war. As you read, ask what each ruler asks of the other, what each claims he cannot do, and what is happening in the background.

The Willy-Nicky Telegrams (July 28 to August 1, 1914)

The Kaiser to the Tsar: It is with the gravest concern that I hear of the impression which Austria's action against Serbia is creating in your country. I beg you to remember that Russia can remain a spectator of the conflict without drawing Europe into the most terrible war it has ever seen. Willy.

The Tsar to the Kaiser: I am glad that you are back. In this serious moment I appeal to you to help me. A shameful war has been declared on a weak country. The indignation in Russia is enormous. I foresee that very soon I shall be overwhelmed by the pressure put upon me, and be forced to take extreme measures that will lead to war. Nicky.

The Tsar to the Kaiser: The military measures which have now come into force were decided five days ago, for defense against Austria's preparations. I hope with all my heart that these measures will not interfere with your part as mediator. Nicky.
